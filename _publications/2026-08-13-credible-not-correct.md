Laypeople increasingly turn to large language models to resolve real legal
problems, against a backdrop of persistent access-to-justice deficits. This
article argues that the practical force of AI-generated legal advice depends not
on its accuracy but on the social production of its credibility.

Drawing on a dual-method analysis of 153 Reddit narratives and 5,341 community
reactions, we map a spectrum of verification practices. A minority of users
triangulate across models, and some submit machine-generated guidance to platform
communities for evaluation before acting — a configuration we term *distributed
counsel*. Far more commonly, narratives are silent on verification altogether:
advice is acted on for its lawyer-like form and emotional reassurance alone.

AI-assisted legal self-help is emerging as an informal infrastructure that
redistributes the work of verification onto those least equipped to bear it.
