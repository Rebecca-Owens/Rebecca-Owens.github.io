Governments are increasingly regulating digital platforms to reduce online harms,
particularly those affecting children — but access restrictions can alter user
behaviour and introduce new privacy and security risks.

This paper uses the phased rollout of the UK Online Safety Act as a natural
experiment, analysing Reddit discourse across VPN and UK Politics communities
alongside a privacy-policy risk analysis of 69 VPN services. Each regulatory
milestone produced significant stepwise increases in VPN-related discussion, with
users framing their response around privacy, surveillance, and distrust of
age-verification intermediaries rather than simple access-seeking.

Demand rose across low, medium, and high-risk providers alike, with the
proportional distribution remaining broadly stable — suggesting that online safety
regulation can create secondary privacy costs even where it does not
disproportionately push users toward riskier services.
